Install

The booting process.

The USB drive is a one-time installer, not the server itself. It carries your phone-signed certificate, turns a spare machine into your Flagship server, and then it's done — you can wipe and reuse it.

STEP 01

Assemble the drive

Flagship Assembler takes three things — your certificate (the JSON your phone signed), a recommended Linux image, and a USB drive — and writes a self-contained boot disk. Your certificate is baked inside the installer image, so the drive needs nothing from the network to know which server it's setting up.

The drive is erased. Everything currently on the USB stick is overwritten. Use a spare one.
STEP 02

Boot the target machine

Plug the drive into the computer you want to become your server and turn it on, selecting the USB drive as the boot device. The installer runs unattended — it partitions the disk, installs the base system, and sets up the Flagship daemon. No keyboard babysitting.

STEP 03

Register, unattended

On first boot the new server generates its own identity, presents your phone's signed certificate, and registers its subdomain. From this moment TLS terminates on your box — flagship.services becomes a dumb pipe that literally cannot read your content.

STEP 04

Reclaim the drive

Once the server is live, the boot drive has done its job. The certificate it carried is single-use and time-limited, so an old drive can't be replayed. Reformat the stick and use it for anything you like.
