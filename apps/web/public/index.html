§1
How it works

Four steps. About ten minutes.

You bring the hardware and a mobile device. Everything else compresses into a USB stick, a QR scan, and a quiet daemon.

01 · Pair

Mobile app is the keychain.

Secure Enclave (iOS) or StrongBox (Android) holds the only key. Lose your device without cloud recovery and the data's gone — like a hardware wallet.

02 · Boot

One USB, one reboot.

Personalized ISO to a stick. Disk encrypts on first boot; the mobile app signs the unlock. Subdomain comes up.

03 · Build

Vibe-code an app.

Describe what you want. LLM emits manifest, Dockerfile, source; the daemon commits, builds, ships in two minutes.

04 · Share

Hand a friend a link.

Every app lives at app.you.flagship.services. One tap to share, one tap to revoke.

§2
Trust model

We structurally cannot read your data.

If we are compromised, all we can leak is your username. That isn't a promise, it's a property of the architecture.

flagshipserver.com handles identity — a username and a sealed blob it can't read. flagship.services is a dumb pipe that routes raw TCP by SNI. TLS terminates on your hardware, with a Let's Encrypt certificate signed by a key only your daemon holds.

Mobile devices initiate every consequential action. Servers never call out unsolicited. No shared database in a third party decides who can see what — every authorization is enforced by the data's own server.

The green padlock in your browser is real. It's signed by a key that lives on your hardware. Nobody else holds a copy.

§4
Questions

Honest questions, honest answers.

The questions that actually matter before you give us your trust. No marketing-speak, no clichés.

Why not just use iCloud, Google Drive, or Dropbox?

Because they read your data. We don't, and structurally we can't: TLS terminates on your hardware. That's the whole product.

What happens when I lose my mobile device?

If you set up cloud recovery (a passkey wraps your master key, ciphertext lives on .com), your new device unwraps it. If you didn't, the data's gone — like a hardware wallet that lost its seed phrase.

Do I need to be a developer?

No. The mobile app walks you through every step. Vibe-coding means typing what you want; the LLM writes it, the daemon ships it.

Can I run this on a Raspberry Pi?

Current image is x86_64. NUCs, mini-PCs, old laptops work. ARM64 is on the list; Pi support tracks the same image.

Is this open source?

Yes. BUSL-1.1, with automatic conversion to Apache 2.0 on May 3, 2030. Daemon, image-builder, mobile clients, webapp, protocol — all public.

What if Flagship the company goes away?

Your box keeps working. Your cert keeps renewing as long as Let's Encrypt is up. We can't lock you in because, structurally, we can't.

How does sharing work without a central account system?

Each pod issues signed invite tokens — short-lived, revocable, scoped to one app. Authorization is enforced by the data's own server.
