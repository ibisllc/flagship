How it works

Three steps. About ten minutes.

You bring the hardware and the phone. We compress everything else into a USB stick, a QR scan, and a vibe.

01 — Pair

Your phone is the keychain.

No cloud account. No email-and-password. No "what was your first pet" recovery questions. Your phone's Secure Enclave (or StrongBox on Android) holds the only key that can boot your server. Lose the phone? You recover from iCloud Keychain or Google Block Store — or you don't recover, and the data is gone, like a hardware wallet. We tell you that clearly during setup.

02 — Boot

One USB. One reboot.

Plug in any old desktop, an Intel NUC, or a box we ship. Boot the personalized ISO once. The disk encrypts itself, your phone signs the unlock, the daemon brings up your subdomain.

03 — Build

Vibe-code an app.

From the phone: "a habit tracker, last 30 days as boxes, no signup." The LLM emits manifest + Dockerfile + source; the daemon commits to your private Forgejo, builds, and ships.

04 — Share

Hand a friend a link.

Every app gets app.you.flagship.services. Share with one tap; revoke with one tap. The marketplace lists what others built and what their security scan turned up — install in two taps, run on your hardware, never trust a third party.

Trust model

We structurally cannot read your data.

flagshipserver.com handles identity. flagship.services is a dumb pipe — bytes routed by SNI. The TLS session terminates on your hardware, with a Let's Encrypt certificate signed by a key only your daemon holds.

If our infrastructure is compromised tomorrow, the worst we can leak about you is your username. That's not a promise about our diligence — it's a property of the architecture.

FAQ

Honest questions, honest answers.

The questions that actually matter before you give us your phone and your hardware. No marketing-speak, no clichés.

Why not just use iCloud, Google Drive, or Dropbox?

Because they read your data. Some of it for product features, some for ads, some — at minimum — for compliance reads. We don't, and structurally we can't: TLS terminates on your hardware. That's the whole product.

What happens when I lose my phone?

If you set up cloud recovery (a passkey wraps your master key, ciphertext lives on .com), your new phone can recover. If you didn't, your data is gone — like a hardware wallet that has lost its seed phrase. Setup tells you this in plain English.

Cloud recovery is opt-in because some users specifically want the "cannot be coerced" property. Both choices are first-class.

Do I need to be a developer?

No. The phone walks you through every step. Vibe-coding an app means typing what you want; the LLM writes it, the daemon ships it. You'll learn what a Postgres table is by accident, not on purpose.

Can I run this on a Raspberry Pi?

The current image is x86_64 only — anything that can run Alpine + Docker compose works (NUCs, mini-PCs, old laptops, that desktop in your closet). An ARM64 image is on the list; Pi support tracks the same image.

If you want plug-and-go, the pre-built box ships fanless and idles at 6 W.

Is this open source?

Yes. BUSL-1.1, with an automatic conversion to Apache 2.0 on May 3, 2030. The protocol package, daemon, image-builder, mobile clients, and webapp are all in the public repository — audit it, build it, run it.

Reproducible builds for the Alpine ISO ship before public launch so you can verify that the bytes you booted match the source.

How does the marketplace work?

Anyone can list an app. We host a description, screenshots, and a link to the canonical pod. We don't host code or data. Listings can pay for a security scan (npm-audit + Trivy + Semgrep + dynamic checks; you get a letter grade A–F). They can also pay to be featured — clearly labeled as "promoted" everywhere.

What if Flagship the company goes away?

Your box keeps working. Your apps keep running. Your TLS cert keeps renewing as long as Let's Encrypt is up and your DNS still points home. We don't lock you in because, structurally, we can't. The license conversion in 2030 is the long-term insurance: by then the protocol is Apache 2.0 even if we're not around to maintain it.

How does sharing work without a central account system?

Each pod issues signed invite tokens — short-lived, revocable, scoped to one app. The recipient's pod stores the token; subsequent requests carry it. There is no shared database of "who can see what" sitting in a third party — every authorization decision is enforced by the data's own server.

What's the "vibe-coding" thing?

You describe an app to your phone. An LLM streams a manifest + Dockerfile + source. The daemon commits to your private Forgejo (yes, you have one), builds the container, deploys it on a fresh subdomain, and tells you the URL. The whole loop is typically two minutes for a small app.

You bring the LLM key (any provider — Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Ollama, OpenRouter), or you can grab a tiny promo allowance to bootstrap.
