Setup & install

Plug in any old PC. Boot from one USB stick. Pair with your phone. Most people are online in about ten minutes.

What hardware do I actually need?

Any x86_64 machine with at least 2 GB of RAM, 32 GB of disk, and a working ethernet port. An old laptop, a NUC, an Optiplex from 2014, our pre-built box — all fine. The builder handles the OS for you, so you don't need to know or care what it runs.

How long does install take?

About ten minutes from inserting the USB stick to a real green padlock at you.flagship.services. Most of that is the kernel booting and Let's Encrypt issuing your cert. To make the stick, pair the app directly with Flagship Studio; your phone sends the signed recipe and Studio does the rest.

Will it wipe the disk?

Yes. The installer formats the target disk, sets up LUKS full-disk encryption with a key only your phone can release, and lays down a hardened Linux base. Anything that was on the disk before is gone. The installer asks twice and shows you the device path; we try hard not to surprise anyone.

Do I need a static IP or port forwarding at home?

No. Your box opens an outbound WebSocket to flagship.services; that's the tunnel. No port-forwarding, no dynamic DNS, no router config. CGNAT is fine. The only requirement is that outbound TCP/443 works, which it does on every consumer ISP.

Can I install without a phone?

Not really, but you have options. The phone is the trust root in the strong-security path — there's no email-and-password to fall back to. If you don't want to use a phone, the installable web app at web.flagshipserver.com works as a peer device (passphrase + WebAuthn instead of Secure Enclave). It's a weaker key-storage trade-off but it's an account you fully control.

How does Studio pairing work?

Open Flagship Studio on your computer. It shows a QR and a short code. In the mobile app, choose Add server → Pair with Studio, scan or enter the code, and compare the security code on both screens. Your phone signs the recipe and sends it directly to Studio; the pairing session is short-lived and single-use. The webapp offers a signed recipe download instead.

Can I run it on a Raspberry Pi?

Not yet. Today the builder targets x86_64 machines; we don't ship an ARM build of the base OS as a default. The daemon itself is plain TypeScript, so a Pi 4 with an SSD is technically capable — if you get there by hand, please tell us how it went.

Privacy & security

The structural answer to "can you read my data?" is no — the bytes never leave your box decrypted. Here's what that means in practice.

Can Flagship read my data?

No, and structurally we can't. TLS terminates on your hardware against a Let's Encrypt cert that only your box holds the private key for. flagship.services sees encrypted bytes go past on a TCP pipe. flagshipserver.com only ever sees your username, your sealed LUKS blob (useless without your phone), and routing metadata. If we get hacked tomorrow, the worst thing leaked about you is your username.

What does Flagship the company actually store?

Username, your server's public key, a sealed LUKS-unlock blob (encrypted to your phone), DNS records for your.flagship.services, push-notification tokens, billing data if you paid us money. That's it. No file content, no prompts, no chats, no service traffic. The full inventory lives in /privacy.

What if my phone is compromised?

Game over for that account, the same way it's game over for your bank app. The phone is the trust root; a compromised root can sign whatever it wants. That's why every key sits in the Secure Enclave / StrongBox behind a biometric, never leaves the chip unwrapped, and every sensitive action requires a fresh biometric prompt. If you suspect a compromise, use any other paired device to revoke the phone's identity key.

What encryption do you use?

Standard primitives only — Ed25519 signatures, X25519 + AES-256-GCM for sealed blobs, HKDF-SHA-256 for key derivation, LUKS2/argon2id for the disk. Real Let's Encrypt certs (TLS-ALPN-01 today, DNS-01 wildcard rolling out) for transport. If we ever invent our own crypto, please report it as a bug.

Is the source open?

Yes. The protocol, daemon, image-builder, and clients are at github.com/ibisllc/flagship under BUSL-1.1, which converts to Apache 2.0 in 2030. Reproducible builds for the ISO are on the roadmap so you can verify the bytes match the source.

Can a court compel you to hand over my data?

They can compel us to hand over what we have, and what we have is a username, a sealed blob, and routing metadata. We can't decrypt your traffic because we don't terminate it. We can revoke the routing entry for your subdomain (which takes your services offline) — that's a power we have and we'd resist abusing. We'll publish a transparency report annually once we have any requests to report.

What do you log?

Cloudflare gets normal request logs (IP, path, status) for flagshipserver.com on a 30-day window — we don't extend them. Fly gets TCP connection metadata for flagship.services. Your daemon logs locally on your box and never ships those logs anywhere. We don't run analytics, ad pixels, or session-replay on the marketing site or the dashboard.

Services

Services are containers running on your box, namespaced by user.

What is "vibe-coding" a service?

You describe what you want in plain language ("a tracker for which houseplants I've watered, push me when one's been thirsty 5+ days"). An LLM writes the code. Your daemon builds it, picks the data-layer scopes (Postgres tables, object storage, push), and runs it at plant-water.you.flagship.services. About 90 seconds. You can edit the code if you feel like it.

Can I share a service with a friend?

Yes. Generate a signed invite token from your phone; your friend's box pulls the source from your pod and runs its own copy under their namespace. Their data stays on their box; yours stays on yours. No central database, no shared backend.

What stops a malicious service from stealing my data?

The data-layer is per-service and per-user-namespaced — a service declares which Postgres tables and which object-storage buckets it wants in its manifest, and the daemon enforces it. A service for "plant watering" can't read your photos. Outbound network access is allowlisted in the manifest; the browser feature has a domain gate. Nothing stops a malicious service from being terrible inside its own scope, which is why you should think before installing one.

Which AI providers can services use?

Whichever you've configured. BYOK to Anthropic, OpenAI, Mistral, anyone with an API. Keys live on your daemon, encrypted at rest, scoped per service. Services never see the raw key. We also ship a one-shot promo path that shows a fresh key at first run for the supported providers — useful for "try Flagship without a credit card" but cosmetically rate-limited.

Can I uninstall a service cleanly?

Yes. Uninstall drops its container, its Postgres schema, its object-storage bucket, its DNS entry, and its cert. You can keep a snapshot before uninstall if you want to come back to the data later.

Money & subscriptions

Hardware is one-time. Subscriptions are optional. Bring-your-own-hardware is free forever.

What does Flagship cost?

Bring-your-own-hardware: $0. We're not selling boxes or subscriptions yet — when we are, we'll publish the details and they'll be opt-in for power-user surfaces only. Most people pay nothing, forever.

Why charge anything if it's "your hardware"?

Three real costs: shipping the pre-built boxes (margin, not profit-from-pricing), running the tunnel hub on Fly (a few cents per server per month at our scale), and running the registrar of flagship.services wildcard DNS. The free tier covers the steady-state for an individual on their own hardware. Subscriptions cover the things we'd otherwise have to subsidize.

Can I cancel anytime?

Yes. Cancellation is one tap from the phone. We prorate the unused portion of the current period. Your subdomain stays yours; the routing entry continues to work; only the billed add-ons (push at scale, peer-backup pool, etc.) shut off. We don't ransom your DNS.

What's your refund policy on hardware?

30 days, full refund, you ship it back. We pay return shipping inside the US; outside the US it's on you. If the box arrived dead or DOA we pay both ways. We don't restock-fee. Full terms at /terms.

Do you take crypto?

Not yet. Stripe takes cards (USD, EUR, GBP). We're exploring monero / lightning for the privacy-conscious, but it's not on the immediate roadmap. If this matters to you, mail support@flagshipserver.com and tell us — that's how features get prioritized.

Will the price change?

Hardware: probably, with component costs. Existing orders are locked in. Subscriptions: we'll grandfather every existing subscriber on a price change for at least 12 months and we'll tell you at signup if a plan is "introductory." We don't do "your renewal is now triple."

How do you make money long-term?

Hardware margin, subscriptions on the optional add-ons. We don't sell ads, telemetry, model-training rights, or your data — partly because it's the right thing, mostly because we structurally can't.

If Flagship goes away

A fair question. The product is designed so this is survivable.

What happens to my server if you shut down?

It keeps running. Your services keep running. Your TLS cert keeps renewing through Let's Encrypt as long as DNS resolves. The two things that would break: routing through flagship.services (because the tunnel hub is ours) and the build pipeline at flagshipserver.com (because that's our Worker). The fix in both cases is a self-hosting playbook we've already drafted — you'd run your own services-zone or point your subdomain at your own IP via DDNS.

What about my data and identity?

The data is on your disk; it was never anywhere else. The identity is your phone's keypair; it doesn't depend on us being alive. The username at flagshipserver.com goes away if the company does, but the underlying public key is yours forever. Migrating to a self-hosted control plane would be one DNS change away.

Will the source survive you?

Yes. BUSL-1.1 with a Change Date of 2030-05-03 — even if the company evaporates tomorrow, the entire codebase becomes Apache 2.0 in 2030 by license, and the GitHub mirror stays up regardless. We'll publish escrow instructions for the build pipeline before public launch.

Didn't find it?

Mail support@flagshipserver.com — a real person reads everything. For broken installs, try /help first; for security questions, /security goes deeper than this page does.
